Closing the Gap: Rebuilding the Sunflower Cybercity Against Its Reference

The Sunflower Cybercity has existed in two forms for a while now. There is the artwork, hero-cybercity.jpg, the hovering car on a neon road between sunflower fields, driving toward a skyline under a striped retro sun. And there is the 3D experiment, a live Three.js scene you can actually fly through. The artwork had detail, warmth, and composition. The 3D scene had motion. What it did not have was fidelity to its own reference.

This phase closed that gap.

Analyse First, Execute Second

The workflow for this phase deserves its own note, because it used two different Claude models for two different jobs.

The analysis was done with Claude Fable. Before a single line of code changed, Fable evaluated the existing scene against the reference image and produced a findings list: dead geometry code in the sunflower field, a phantom shadow pipeline with no shadow-casting lights, an unbounded texture scroll offset that would degrade over long sessions, roughly 1,700 transparent instances causing overdraw, a sun gradient that was inverted relative to the artwork, flat unlit boxes where the reference showed glowing windowed towers, and a flat navy sky where the artwork has a magenta horizon.

Execution then continued with Sonnet, working from that findings list as its brief. The split matters: the evaluation phase is where the expensive judgement lives, deciding what is actually wrong and what is worth fixing, while the implementation phase is long, mechanical, and well-specified once the brief exists. Matching the model to the nature of the work, deeper analysis up front, sustained execution after, is the same context engineering principle this whole project runs on, applied one level down at the model-selection layer.

What Changed in the Scene

Geometry. Flower heads are now true merged petal geometry, 14 petal discs around a dark centre, with per-instance colour variation and wind sway injected directly into the vertex shader. The field follows a power-law distribution biased toward the road shoulder, 2,500 instances strong, with oversized hero sunflowers cropped into the bottom corners exactly as the artwork frames them. The mountains became noise-displaced, vertex-coloured peaks fading from dark purple to pink. The six hardcoded laser beams became a 16-beam procedurally seeded lattice.

Textures. Buildings gained procedural lit-window emissive maps, warm yellow and cyan windows with magenta billboard blocks, generated with OffscreenCanvas. The road was redrawn with glowing cyan edge lines, a dashed centre, and faint scanlines. The sky is now a canvas-gradient horizon, indigo into magenta. The sun gradient was flipped to match the reference: golden at the top, deep orange at the base, with pink banding below.

Rendering. Tone mapping moved to ACESFilmic with WebGL2 MSAA on the composer. Resolution now adapts live, scaling from real FPS via a performance monitor plus navigator.deviceMemory and hardwareConcurrency heuristics that size the instance counts to the device. The scene respects prefers-reduced-motion, freezing the sway and the pulse for users who ask for stillness. And the ship now takes Gamepad API input, so a controller's left stick steers the same drive the keyboard always did.

Verified, Not Assumed

The loop did not end at "it builds". The scene was rendered headlessly in Chromium, screenshotted, and compared against the reference image, then iterated until the capture genuinely resembled the artwork. The experiment's preview thumbnail is no longer an approximation; it is a real frame from the rebuilt scene.

The Sunflower Cybercity started as a poem, became an artwork, became a video, and has been a navigable place all along. Now the place finally looks like the picture.
