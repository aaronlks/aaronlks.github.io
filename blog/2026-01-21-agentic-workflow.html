Embracing the Agentic Workflow

Status: Living Document | Last updated: 2026-09-29

After stumbling upon Google Antigravity, I turned a site refresh into a Digital Creative Playground built within the Sunflower Cybercity paradigm. By applying prior web architecture experience to the modern AI workflow, I distilled development into precise domain knowledge that lets me direct the AI with exact intent, moving beyond manual coding into symbiotic co-authoring.

What is it?

I define the intent and context, the "why" and "what", and let an AI agent handle the execution, the "how". This covers architectural reasoning, design implementation, and rapid iteration, not just code generation.

The Process

  1. Context Engineering: Provided the agent with a semantic mental framework distilled from prior professional experience.
  2. Iterative Refinement: Moved from broad strokes (layout, color palette) to fine details (shimmer effects, routing).
  3. Symbiosis: Focused on creative direction and content strategy while the agent handled React components, Tailwind styling, and routing logic.

The Ecosystem

Core Agents

  • Antigravity Agent (The Architect): Powered by Gemini 3 Pro, responsible for orchestration, build processes, and context awareness.
  • Context Engineer (The User): Responsible for high-level vision, aesthetic direction, and steering the agent via natural language.

The Rules Mechanism

A formal Rules Mechanism (docs/RULES.md) keeps the project a "living" entity:

  • Documentation is Code: Key docs (agents.md, prompts.md) are treated as source of truth.
  • Synchronization: Walkthrough and prompt history updated with every significant change.
  • Context Loading: Agent reads rules and latest blog posts at the start of every session.

38. Hero Copy Refinement (Phase 62)

Updated the Hero copy in hero.json and the matching summary in public/cms/resume.json.

39. Sunflower Cybercity Scene Overhaul (Phase 64)

The 3D experiment was evaluated against its reference artwork (hero-cybercity.jpg) and rebuilt to close the gap: merged-petal flower geometry with vertex-shader wind sway, procedural lit-window textures for buildings generated with OffscreenCanvas, a re-graded sun shader, noise-displaced mountains, a canvas-gradient sky and a procedurally seeded laser lattice. Rendering leans on web platform APIs: ACESFilmic tone mapping with WebGL2 MSAA, adaptive resolution from live FPS plus Device Memory and Hardware Concurrency heuristics, prefers-reduced-motion support, and Gamepad API controls. Verified by screenshotting the scene headlessly against the reference, and the experiment's preview asset was regenerated from a real capture.

40. Cybercity Rebuild Blog Post & Two-Model Workflow Note (Phase 65)

New blog post: "Closing the Gap: Rebuilding the Sunflower Cybercity Against Its Reference", documenting the Phase 64 overhaul with the reference artwork and a live capture side by side. It also notes the two-model workflow behind the phase: one model produced the scene evaluation and findings list, and a second carried out the implementation from that brief.

41. Repo Housekeeping (Phases 66-67)

A standalone subproject that had been kept alongside the site was removed from this repo, and docs/RULES.md was revised to scope the repo to the site itself. The site's code and publishing pipeline are unchanged; the build was re-verified after the removal.

42. Sunflower Cybercity Composition Pass (Phase 68)

The scene was re-checked against its reference by rendering it headlessly first and reading the capture beside the artwork before opening any source. Each part built in Phase 64 was correct; the composition they added up to was not. The sky's teal horizon stop sat below the ground plane and was never visible, the sun clipped to a flat white disc under bloom, the car covered the sun, the field read as a single row on the horizon, and the foreground blooms sat outside the camera's field of view.

The fixes were mostly framing. The sky gradient and fog were re-mapped to a shared teal horizon, the city's inner edge became an angular corridor that keeps a wedge of sky open, and three components arrived: a tiled sunflower carpet, elevated monorail guideways and star flares. Petals, laser beams and the car were rebuilt. Several real bugs surfaced along the way and were fixed, including a frustum-culling error on instanced buildings, a fractional road-texture repeat, and a screenshot tool that timed out on animated canvases. The preview image was re-encoded from PNG to JPEG, cutting it from 1.72 MB to 268 KB. New blog post: "Reading the Frame: What a Screenshot Knows That the Source Does Not".

43. Pollen Drift, and a Library Evaluated Then Rejected (Phase 72)

The scene gained drifting pollen over the sunflower field. The first attempt used Spark, a 3D Gaussian Splatting renderer for three.js, driven with procedurally generated splats rather than a captured asset. It built and ran cleanly and drew nothing: the splats only appeared with the post-processing chain bypassed, and that chain is not optional here, its bloom is what makes the sun, the neon lattice and the car's lights read. It also added 4.72 MB to the experiment's bundle. Spark was removed and the effect was rebuilt directly with sprites carrying the same falloff, each mote drifting on its own phase, for under three kilobytes.

Checking the result surfaced an unrelated bug dating from the Phase 68 rebuild: the flower heads were handed a new material list on every redraw, so the field was rebuilt without its placement data and stripped to bare stems whenever the scene lowered its own resolution. Fixed, and the capture regenerated.

44. Homepage Date Fix & In-Scene Versioning (Phase 74)

The homepage's Sunflower Cybercity card still showed its launch date no matter how much the scene changed underneath it, and the experiment page gave no indication of when it was last touched. The card now shows the date of the most recent real change to the scene, and the experiment page gained a second HUD line reporting the same date.

45. Re-Evaluating the Scene: Sky, Lattice and Field (Phase 75)

A second capture-first pass, again reading the rendered frame beside the reference before touching any source. The sky's teal band was still hidden behind the towers, the far skyline's height cap had never been applied so a tower and its mast stood through the sun, and the laser lattice had lost its mirror symmetry to random endpoints. The field also read brown from near-black seed heads and unlit stems, and the previous phase's pollen was too faint to see in a capture. Each was fixed, with the lattice rebuilt as mirrored pairs, which took three passes to place. The preview image and last-updated tag were regenerated.

46. Skyline Set Back, and a Scored Rubric (Phase 76)

This pass added a fixed scoring rubric to the capture loop: composition, lighting, materials and details, out of ten, adapted from dream-loop, an open-source agent skill for iterating visuals toward a target image. The baseline scored about 4.3, with composition the biggest loss: the flanking towers formed a wall across both sides of the frame, while the reference stands its skyline on the horizon behind the field. Towers are now sized by their on-screen height, which restored sky across the full width and brought the elevated rails, star flares and laser lattice back into view. The car was moved closer and its exhaust rebuilt as fading cyan and magenta streaks. Final score about 6.6.
